RAMP-UP DIAGNOSTIC

Where does the health response accelerate?

The breakpoint is a continuous two-slope fit searched over the middle 60% of observed exposure values. The interval is a bootstrap diagnostic, not an official hazard threshold. Peak-HI plots also show the current LIX 108°F advisory and 113°F warning criteria for reference.

Pearson r is deliberately left beside the nonlinear view: it summarizes the overall linear association, while the dark curve shows the locally weighted mean response and the shaded band shows an approximate pointwise 95% local uncertainty interval. A close ranking is not proof that one variable is meaningfully better, and none of these unadjusted plots establishes causation.

Outdoor WBGT is now included. It is calculated with the Liljegren method using ERA5-Land/ERA5 temperature, humidity, pressure, wind, shortwave radiation and solar geometry. Direct radiation is used when available and otherwise estimated from global radiation. The daily regional value is the mean of configured parish-point daily maximum WBGT values. ADJUSTED MODEL

Population-normalized nonlinear model comparison

These archive-level models use region-day ED counts with a log(population) offset, a cubic spline for each exposure, and adjustment for year, month, day-of-week, plus region in the combined-CWA model. Negative binomial is preferred; Poisson is used only if that fit fails. Lower ΔAIC indicates better relative fit. The rate ratio compares the model response at the 90th exposure percentile with the median.

This seasonal split can flag timing differences worth investigating, but by itself it does not prove acclimatization; the heat-index distribution and other seasonal factors also change.

CDC connection: CDC has used each HHS region's 95th percentile of daily warm-season heat-related ED visit rates to identify unusually high health-impact days. This page adapts that percentile idea to the local LDH series, then asks which local peak-HI thresholds capture those days. The numbers are not directly mixed with CDC rates because CDC reports HRI visits per 100,000 all-cause ED visits, while this local LDH analysis uses visits per 100,000 population. CDC methodology.

Operational interpretation: a lower candidate trigger can increase sensitivity while also increasing the number of flagged days. This section quantifies that tradeoff; it does not by itself define a new NWS advisory criterion.
